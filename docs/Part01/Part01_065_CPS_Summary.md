# Summary

Cyber-physical systems integrate computation with physical processes. Embedded and potentially networked computers monitor and control these processes using sensor measurements and computational models. The models
and computations may be located within the system or provided by remote backend services.

CPSs feature **tight coupling** between their physical and computational components. Some CPSs must operate autonomously for long periods with limited or no human intervention.

Embedded computers and communication networks monitor and control physical processes, usually through **feedback loops**. Within these loops, measurements of the physical process affect subsequent computations, while the resulting computational decisions influence the physical process.

Embedded systems can also interact directly with the physical world. The distinction is therefore one of emphasis: an embedded-system perspective often focuses on the computational component, whereas a CPS perspective considers the computation, communication, control, and physical process as a single interconnected system.
