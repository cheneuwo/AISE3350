# Vehicle-to-Pedestrian (V2P)

Collisions involving pedestrians and cyclists account for a substantial proportion of traffic fatalities.

[Vehicle-to-pedestrian (V2P)](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/55626) technologies use sensors and communication systems to detect and communicate the presence of at-risk pedestrians or cyclists, potentially reducing collisions involving these vulnerable road users.

## Implementations

The 2019 Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) report considered three categories of pedestrian safety technologies:

- **Vehicle-based:** These systems detect pedestrians and alert drivers through the vehicle or infrastructure. Some systems can also intervene to help prevent a collision if the driver fails to respond adequately. In the FHWA study, two systems were tested in this category: a **camera-based** aftermarket safety device and an integrated pedestrian detection and collision mitigation system using **camera–radar fusion**.

- **Smartphone-based:** These systems detect vehicles and notify pedestrians of potentially dangerous situations through a handheld device, typically the user's mobile phone, potentially with support from roadside infrastructure. In the FHWA study, the software was at an early stage of deployment, and the necessary hardware and software were installed at the test bed.

- **Infrastructure-based:** These systems can provide collision alerts to both drivers and pedestrians, or at least to drivers, by supporting detection and notification through roadside infrastructure. In the FHWA study, no system was acquired for testing in this category because suitable technology was not sufficiently market-ready at the time. However, the researchers conducted a comprehensive investigation of its potential.

:::{note}
The report uses a broad classification of pedestrian safety technologies. A camera-based pedestrian detection system does not necessarily involve direct wireless communication between a vehicle and a pedestrian's device. It is therefore useful to distinguish **pedestrian detection** from **connected V2P communication**, although both can contribute to pedestrian safety.
:::
