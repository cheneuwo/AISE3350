# Level 5: Full Driving Automation

At **Level 5**, an automated driving system performs the **entire dynamic driving task and fallback response under all on-road conditions in which humans can drive**, without requiring human driving.

**When engaged, the system drives and the occupants ride.**

Unlike Level 4, its automated driving capability is not restricted to a particular operational design domain.

## The Human's Role

Occupants are passengers. They do not need to supervise the driving environment, operate vehicle controls, or remain available to take over.

Human interaction may include selecting a destination, changing the route, or requesting a stop. These are trip-management activities rather than performance of the dynamic driving task.

Selecting a destination is therefore one possible interaction—not necessarily the only human involvement.

## The System's Role

The system performs all aspects of driving, including:

- Controlling steering, acceleration, and braking.
- Monitoring and responding to traffic and road conditions.
- Adapting its driving behaviour to changing circumstances.
- Handling fallback without depending on human takeover.

As at Level 4, the system may need to stop rather than continue a journey when circumstances make continued operation inappropriate.

## Operating Conditions

Level 5 extends automated driving beyond the defined operating conditions associated with Level 4. It is not restricted by design to selected neighbourhoods, mapped service areas, or particular weather conditions within the range that human drivers can manage.

However, **Level 5 does not mean that weather has no effect on the vehicle**. Rain, snow, fog, and ice can still affect visibility, traction, and appropriate driving speed. The system must respond appropriately, just as a capable human driver would.

Likewise, Level 5 does not require driving through floods, travelling on impassable roads, or overcoming the vehicle's physical limitations.

:::{important}
**Level 4:** Automated driving and fallback within defined operating conditions.

**Level 5:** Automated driving and fallback under all on-road conditions in which humans can drive.

Neither level guarantees that every journey can be completed regardless of circumstances.
:::

## Geographical Restrictions

A Level 5 driving capability is not inherently limited to a geographical service area. Nevertheless, an operator may restrict where a service is offered for business or regulatory reasons.

A service boundary should therefore be distinguished from a technical limitation of the automated driving system.

## Key Takeaway

**Level 5 removes the restriction to a particular operating domain—not the constraints imposed by physics, road conditions, or safe operation.**

## Discussion

A vehicle can drive without human intervention throughout a city, but its automated system cannot operate outside that mapped area.

- Why does this capability correspond to Level 4 rather than Level 5?
- Would removing the steering wheel change its classification?
- Could a Level 5 system appropriately decide not to continue along a flooded road?
